01 · System Architecture

One ecosystem, deliberately separated responsibilities.

BAIDNET coordinates blockchain-native capabilities and traditional-currency FinTech capabilities without treating them as the same settlement system. The user experience can be integrated while the transaction paths, controls, records, and external responsibilities remain distinct.

02 · Transaction Model

Dual-rail financial architecture.

Blockchain rail

BAIDNET-owned ledger activity follows blockchain transaction controls, signatures, nonce/replay protections, consensus/finality behavior, receipts, and ledger evidence. Supported blockchain interactions remain distinct from USD fiat activity.

Fiat / FinTech rail

The FinTech path uses a separately governed transaction lifecycle designed for sponsor-bank connectivity, including idempotency, callbacks, correlation, reconciliation, audit evidence, and partner-specific production controls.

03 · Core Technology

BAIDNET blockchain.

The blockchain layer provides BAIDNET's native ledger and transaction environment. Engineering evidence reviewed for the platform includes validator/consensus behavior, persistent ledger state, transaction signatures, finality-oriented payment flows, monitoring, and operational runtime components.

Documentation describes implemented engineering scope. It does not represent regulatory approval, sponsor-bank approval, or an independently certified production deployment.

QUAD VM.

QUAD is BAIDNET's execution environment and language/runtime workstream. The implementation history covers language grammar and operators, intermediate representation, interpreter execution, settlement integration, programs/storage/registry/state-root behavior, and later PBFT/persistent-node/runtime work.

Implementation & test evidence represented

BAIDPAY.

BAIDPAY is the ecosystem payment experience. Presented capabilities include wallet/account experiences, peer-to-peer and merchant flows, payment requests, receipts, transaction history, claims/refunds, and blockchain-supported payment interactions while preserving the separate fiat boundary.

FinTech extension.

The FinTech extension adds traditional-currency transaction primitives around a controlled lifecycle rather than collapsing fiat into the blockchain rail. Documented workstreams cover routing, idempotency, callback ingress, event persistence, correlation, audit trails, reconciliation, runtime governance, and regression safety.

Internal engineering

Presented workstreams are reported complete within their documented internal scope, with verification evidence attached to the commercialization materials.

External activation

Sponsor-bank validation, partner-specific configuration, legal classification, production settlement connectivity, applicable approvals, and independent validation remain separate gates.

04 · Evidence Center

Proof is separated from readiness.

Product proof answers “does the technology and workflow exist?” Readiness evidence answers “what has been verified, under what scope, and what still gates deployment?” Keeping those questions separate prevents engineering completion from being overstated as commercial or regulatory approval.

Product proof

Customer/business interfaces, wallet and merchant flows, transaction lifecycle behavior, claims/refunds, governance/reporting functions, mobile access, APIs, blockchain execution, and deployment artifacts.

Readiness evidence

Workstream verification, routing boundaries, callback/replay controls, idempotency, correlation, reconciliation, audit-trail reconstruction, runtime checks, regression tests, and explicitly documented external dependencies.

05 · Readiness Boundary

Ready for examination. Not represented as fully activated production.

The current evidence supports technical due diligence and controlled-pilot preparation. Commercial production depends on external decisions and validations that the codebase cannot complete by itself.

Internal

Engineering evidence

Implementation, internal verification, transaction controls, auditability surfaces, runtime/deployment artifacts, and integration-ready boundaries.

External gates

Deployment authority

Legal classification, sponsor-bank diligence/approval, bank-specific compliance configuration, production settlement validation, independent security review, and any required regulatory or contractual authorization.
